How it works

This is the height of instrument (HI) method. The first row is the benchmark, with the backsight you read on it. The HI is the elevation plus the backsight. Every other point's elevation is the HI minus the rod reading on it.

A row with both readings is a turning point. Its foresight sets its elevation, and then its backsight sets the next HI. A row with only a foresight is a side shot (intermediate sight) and doesn't change the HI. The last row is where the loop closes.

The arithmetic check confirms that the total of the backsights minus the total of the foresights equals the change in elevation. Side shots aren't part of it. Passing it means the math is right, not that the field work is.

If you give the last point's known elevation, the misclosure is spread evenly across the instrument setups. Points read from the first setup get one share of the correction, points from the second setup get two shares, and so on. The last point then lands exactly on its known elevation. For the whole thing worked by hand, plus the two-peg test, see the field note Running a level loop, step by step.
