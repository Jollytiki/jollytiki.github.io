How it works

Enter any two of the eight curve values. Angles take any common format: 45°30'15", 45 30 15, 45-30-15 or decimal degrees. Distances can be in feet or meters, as long as you use the same unit throughout.

Degree of curve uses the arc definition, the central angle for 100 units of arc: D = 5729.578 / R. That's the highway standard in feet.

If you give a chord with the radius, the solver assumes the curve is under 180°. A middle ordinate longer than the radius gives a curve over 180°. When you give two lengths and no angle or radius, like an arc length and a chord, it finds Δ by trial and error and assumes a curve under 180°. A tangent with a middle ordinate always fits two different curves, so that pair isn't accepted.

For curves over 180°, like a cul-de-sac bulb, enter Δ directly. The tangent and external don't exist past 180°, so they show as —.

Stationing follows the usual rule: PC = PI − T and PT = PC + L. The PT is stationed along the arc, so it isn't PI + T. Bearings use the back tangent, which is the direction of travel arriving at the PC. Chord bearing = back tangent ± Δ/2, and ahead tangent = back tangent ± Δ. To stake the curve, see the field note Laying out a curve, step by step.
