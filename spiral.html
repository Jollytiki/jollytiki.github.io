How it works

The spirals are clothoids, where curvature grows evenly from zero at the TS to 1/R at the SC. The spiral angle is θs = Ls / 2R. X and Y are the SC's distance along the tangent and offset from it, from the standard series. p is how far the circular curve is pushed in from the tangent, and k is how far along the tangent the shifted curve's PC would be.

Give either the total Δ (tangent to tangent) or the circular arc length. The arc's own central angle is Δc = Δ − θs(entry) − θs(exit), so Δ has to be at least the two spiral angles combined.

The tangent lengths (TS to PI and PI to ST) and the external come from the actual geometry. That makes them exact for unequal spirals, where the two tangents differ. For equal spirals they match the textbook Ts = (R + p) tan(Δ/2) + k and Es = (R + p) sec(Δ/2) − R.

LT and ST are the spiral's long and short tangents. LC is the spiral's long chord, and the deflection is the angle at the TS from the tangent to the SC, about θs/3. Stationing runs along the curve: TS + Ls = SC, SC + Lc = CS, CS + Ls = ST.
