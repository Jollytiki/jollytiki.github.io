How it works

It's the standard inverse: ΔN = N₂ − N₁, ΔE = E₂ − E₁, distance = √(ΔN² + ΔE²), and azimuth = atan2(ΔE, ΔN), measured clockwise from north. The azimuth is then turned into a quadrant bearing.

The distance is a plane (grid) distance in whatever units your coordinates are in. If you're working in State Plane and need a ground distance, divide by your combined scale factor.

Got two points in the same spot? You'll get a zero distance and no bearing, because a line with no length has no direction.
