How it works

Bearing – Bearing runs a line from each point and finds where they cross. Parallel lines have no answer. If the crossing is behind one of the points (the opposite way from the bearing you gave), you get a note, because that's usually a typo in a bearing.

Distance – Distance swings an arc from each point and finds where they meet. Two arcs usually cross twice, so you get two solutions, one on each side of the line between the points. If the arcs are too far apart, or one sits inside the other, there's no answer.

Bearing – Distance runs a line from point 1 and swings an arc from point 2. The line can cross the arc twice. A solution that falls behind point 1 is marked.

Bearings can be written N 45-30-15 E or N45°30'15"E. A plain number is an azimuth from north. Coordinates can be in feet or meters, as long as every value uses the same unit.
